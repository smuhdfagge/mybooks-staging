A salary in Nigeria is never just salary less tax. Pension comes off it, the
National Housing Fund may, and the employer pays its own share of pension and
two payroll levies on top. Get one of them wrong and every payslip is wrong.

Here is each one: who pays it, how much, and where it goes.

## At a glance

| Deduction | Who pays | Rate | Applies to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pension, employee | Employee | at least 8% | Employers with 15 or more staff |
| Pension, employer | Employer | at least 10% | Employers with 15 or more staff |
| National Housing Fund | Employee | 2.5% | Compulsory in the public sector, voluntary in private employment |
| NSITF employee compensation | Employer | 1% of payroll | All employers |
| ITF training levy | Employer | 1% of payroll | Employers with 5 or more staff, or turnover of ₦50 million or more |
| PAYE | Employee | 0% to 25% | Anyone earning above the minimum wage; see the [PAYE guide](/guides/paye-nigeria) |

## Pension

Under the Pension Reform Act, an employer with **at least 15 employees** must
put them in the contributory pension scheme. Organisations with fewer than
three employees, and the self-employed, can join voluntarily.

- The **minimum contribution is 18%** of monthly emoluments: at least 10% from
  the employer and 8% from the employee.
- An employer can choose to pay all of it, in which case the minimum is **20%**.
- The employee's contribution comes off pay **before PAYE** is worked out, so
  it lowers their tax.
- The employer must also take out **group life insurance** for its staff, for
  at least three times each employee's yearly emoluments.

The money goes to each employee's own Pension Fund Administrator, not to the
government. "Monthly emoluments" here is at least basic salary, housing and
transport; check with your Pension Fund Administrator what else your pay
structure brings in.

## National Housing Fund

The NHF is **2.5%** of the employee's monthly income, deducted by the employer
and paid to the **Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria** within one month.

It is **compulsory in the public sector**. Employees in private businesses are
no longer obliged to contribute and may do so voluntarily. Where a private
employee chooses to, the employer still deducts and remits it, and it comes off
pay before PAYE.

## NSITF: employee compensation

Under the Employee Compensation Act, employers pay **1% of their payroll** to
the Nigeria Social Insurance Trust Fund. It funds compensation for staff injured
at work. Nothing comes off the employee's pay for it.

## ITF: the training levy

The Industrial Training Fund levy is **1% of the employer's yearly payroll**,
paid by employers with five or more employees, or with a turnover of ₦50 million
or more. Like NSITF, it is a cost to the business, not a deduction from pay.

## One payslip, worked through

Tunde's monthly emoluments are ₦400,000: ₦250,000 basic, ₦100,000 housing and
₦50,000 transport. He works for a private company with 40 staff and does not
contribute to the NHF.

| Tunde's payslip | Monthly |
| --- | --- |
| Gross pay | ₦400,000.00 |
| Pension, employee 8% | −₦32,000.00 |
| PAYE (2026 bands, no rent relief) | −₦48,740.00 |
| **Net pay** | **₦319,260.00** |

The PAYE: a year's pay of ₦4,800,000 less ₦384,000 of pension leaves ₦4,416,000
taxable. That is ₦0 on the first ₦800,000, ₦330,000 at 15% on the next
₦2,200,000 and ₦254,880 at 18% on the remaining ₦1,416,000: ₦584,880 a year, or
₦48,740 a month. If Tunde declared rent, rent relief would lower it.

What the company pays on top of his ₦400,000:

| Employer's cost | Monthly |
| --- | --- |
| Pension, employer 10% | ₦40,000.00 |
| NSITF 1% | ₦4,000.00 |
| ITF 1% | ₦4,000.00 |
| **Cost of employing Tunde** | **₦448,000.00** |

## When each is paid

| Deduction | Paid to | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PAYE | State revenue service (NRS for FCT residents) | By the 10th of the next month |
| Pension | The employee's Pension Fund Administrator | Within 7 working days of paying salaries |
| NHF | Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria | Within one month of deduction |
| NSITF | Nigeria Social Insurance Trust Fund | As assessed by the NSITF |
| ITF | Industrial Training Fund | Yearly |

## How MyBooks does it

In MyBooks each deduction is set up once, as a percentage with the base it is
worked out on, and marked as coming off before tax or after. Every pay run then
applies them the same way, prints them on the payslip, and posts the employer's
share as a cost and the amounts owed to each body as liabilities, so you can see
what is due to whom before the date arrives.
