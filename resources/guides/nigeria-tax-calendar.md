Most tax penalties in Nigeria are not for getting a sum wrong. They are for
missing a date. The dates are fixed, the same every year, and most of them come
round every month.

Here they all are.

## Every month

| Date | What is due | Paid to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **10th** | PAYE deducted from last month's salaries | Your employees' state revenue service (NRS for FCT residents) |
| **14th** | VAT withheld by withholding VAT agents (government bodies, banks, oil and gas companies, telecoms) | Nigeria Revenue Service |
| **21st** | VAT return and payment for last month, even if no VAT is due | Nigeria Revenue Service |
| **21st** | Withholding tax deducted last month, from companies | Nigeria Revenue Service |
| **30th** | Withholding tax deducted last month, from individuals and unincorporated businesses | The state revenue service |
| Within one month | National Housing Fund contributions deducted | Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria |

Pension contributions go to each employee's Pension Fund Administrator every
month, as soon as salaries are paid.

The 21st is the busy one. VAT and federal withholding tax share it, so one
afternoon's work clears both.

## Every year

| Date | What is due |
| --- | --- |
| **31 January** | Employer's return of everything paid to each employee in the previous year |
| **31 March** | Individuals' own income tax returns, within 90 days of the year end |
| **Six months after your year end** | Company income tax return, with audited accounts and the tax computation |

For a company whose year ends on **31 December**, the income tax return is due
by **30 June**. A newly incorporated company files within six months of its
year end or 18 months after incorporation, whichever comes first. The NRS can
allow up to two months more if you apply in writing before the deadline.

**Small companies file too.** A company paying 0% income tax still files its
return by the same date. See [what the Nigeria Tax Act
changed](/guides/nigeria-tax-act-small-business).

## A year at a glance

| Month | Dates to watch |
| --- | --- |
| January | 10th PAYE · 21st VAT and WHT · **31st employer's annual return** |
| February | 10th PAYE · 21st VAT and WHT |
| March | 10th PAYE · 21st VAT and WHT · **31st individuals' returns** |
| April to May | 10th PAYE · 21st VAT and WHT, each month |
| June | 10th PAYE · 21st VAT and WHT · **30th company return (31 December year end)** |
| July to December | 10th PAYE · 21st VAT and WHT, each month |

If a date falls on a weekend or public holiday, do not count on it moving. File
before it.

## What a missed date costs

| What | Penalty |
| --- | --- |
| Company income tax return filed late | ₦100,000 for the first month, then ₦50,000 for each further month |
| Any tax paid late | 10% of the unpaid amount, plus interest at the Central Bank's rate plus a margin |
| Withholding tax deducted but not remitted | 10% of the amount not remitted, plus interest |
| Not using e-invoicing when required | ₦200,000 plus 100% of the tax due, plus interest |

PAYE and withholding tax need the most care. That money was taken from someone
else's pay or invoice, and once you have deducted it, the business answers for
it.

## Staying ahead of it

Three habits do most of the work:

1. **Close the month by the 7th.** Payroll posted, sales and purchases in, bank
   reconciled. Then the 10th and the 21st are filing, not finding.
2. **Keep the tax in its own account.** VAT, PAYE and withholding tax collected
   are not your money; moving them aside on the day keeps the 21st affordable.
3. **Diary the annual dates now.** 31 January and six months after your year
   end are easy to forget because they only come once.
