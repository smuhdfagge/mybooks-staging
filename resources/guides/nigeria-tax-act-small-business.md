Nigeria's tax laws were rewritten in 2025, and the new Acts have applied since
**1 January 2026**. The Federal Inland Revenue Service is now the **Nigeria
Revenue Service (NRS)**, and several taxes a small business pays, or no longer
pays, changed with it.

This is what changed, and what it means for a business of your size.

## Small companies pay 0% company income tax

A company is **small** when both of these are true:

- yearly gross turnover of **₦100 million or less**, and
- total fixed assets of **no more than ₦250 million**.

Firms that provide professional services, such as law, accounting and
consultancy practices, cannot count as small, however small they are.

A small company's income tax rate is **0%**. Every other company pays **30%**.

0% is not the same as nothing to do. **Every company still files its tax return
every year**, small or not. A return that shows ₦0 due is still a return, and
the penalty for not filing it applies all the same.

## A 4% development levy replaces four old ones

Companies that are not small now pay a single **development levy of 4%** of
their assessable profits. It replaces:

- Tertiary Education Tax (3%)
- the NITDA IT levy (1%)
- the NASENI levy (0.25%, for some sectors)
- the Police Trust Fund levy

Small companies do not pay it.

## PAYE: new bands, no consolidated relief

For employees, income tax now runs from **0% on the first ₦800,000** a year to
**25% above ₦50 million**. The consolidated relief allowance is gone. In its
place, an employee can claim **rent relief** of 20% of the rent they pay, up to
₦500,000 a year. Anyone earning the minimum wage or less pays no PAYE.

If your payroll was set up before 2026, it needs redoing. The [PAYE
guide](/guides/paye-nigeria) has the bands and a worked example.

## VAT: same rate, better recovery

- The rate is still **7.5%**.
- **Businesses turning over less than ₦50 million a year do not charge VAT**
  or file VAT returns. Note this is a different line from the ₦100 million
  small company test above.
- Basic food, medical and pharmaceutical products, educational books and
  materials, and exported services are **zero-rated**.
- VAT paid on **services and fixed assets** used to make taxable supplies can
  now be recovered against the VAT you charge. Before, much of it could not.
- Input VAT can be claimed up to **five years** after the period it was paid in.

The return is still due by the **21st** of the following month. The [VAT
returns guide](/guides/vat-returns-nigeria) walks through it.

## E-invoicing is now law

The new laws require businesses to use the NRS's fiscalisation system: invoices
are reported to the NRS, which validates them. It is being brought in by size of
business, largest first. Not complying carries a fine of **₦200,000 plus 100% of
the tax due, plus interest**.

The [e-invoicing guide](/guides/e-invoicing-nigeria) explains when it reaches
you and what to do first.

## Withholding tax: small companies can be paid in full

A small company with a valid TIN should not have withholding tax deducted from
payments to it, as long as a single customer pays it **less than ₦2 million**
in a month. The [withholding tax guide](/guides/withholding-tax-nigeria) has
the rates and the exception.

## Late filing and late payment cost more

| What | Penalty |
| --- | --- |
| Company income tax return filed late | ₦100,000 for the first month, then ₦50,000 for each further month |
| Any tax paid late | 10% of the unpaid amount, plus interest |
| Not using e-invoicing when required | ₦200,000 plus 100% of the tax due, plus interest |

## What to do now

- Check your turnover and fixed assets against the small company test
- Put your return date in the diary even if you will owe nothing: six months
  after your year end
- Update payroll to the 2026 PAYE bands and collect rent declarations
- Get valid TINs from your suppliers and give yours to your customers
- Find out when e-invoicing reaches your size of business
