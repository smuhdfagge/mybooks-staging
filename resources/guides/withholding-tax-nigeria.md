Withholding tax confuses people because it is not really a tax. It is an advance
payment of someone else's tax that you are made to collect.

When you pay a supplier, you keep back a slice, send it to the tax office, and
give the supplier a credit note for it. They use that note against their own tax
bill. If you forget to deduct it, the tax office comes to you, not to them.

## Who has to deduct

The duty falls on **companies and other organisations**, not on private
individuals. That means:

- Limited companies and other corporate bodies
- Unincorporated businesses, partnerships and firms
- Government ministries, departments and agencies
- Tax-exempt bodies too: NGOs, charities and cooperatives

A person paying a builder to work on their own house, in a purely private
capacity, is not a withholding agent. A business paying the same builder is.

## The rates

These are the rates for payments to people and companies resident in Nigeria,
under the Deduction of Tax at Source (Withholding) Regulations.

| What you are paying for | Company | Individual |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Construction of roads, bridges, buildings, power plants | 2% | 2% |
| Other construction and related work | 5% | 5% |
| Professional, consultancy, technical and management fees | 5% | 5% |
| Commission and brokerage | 5% | 5% |
| Rent, hire or lease | 10% | 10% |
| Dividends and interest | 10% | 10% |
| Royalties | 10% | 5% |
| Supply of goods, where the supplier did not make them | 2% | 2% |
| Other services not listed above | 2% | 2% |
| Director's fees | — | 15% |

Two things catch people out here.

**Construction is split.** Building a road, a bridge, a building or a power
plant is 2%. Other construction and related work is 5%. Which one a job falls
under is worth getting right before you pay, not after.

**No TIN means a higher rate.** Where the supplier cannot give you a valid Tax
Identification Number, the rate is doubled. This is the clearest reason to
collect TINs from suppliers before you pay them rather than afterwards.

## Work it out on the amount before VAT

This is the mistake that costs money. WHT is calculated on the value of the
supply, **not on the VAT-inclusive total**.

A consultant invoices you ₦1,000,000 plus 7.5% VAT:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Services | ₦1,000,000.00 |
| VAT at 7.5% | ₦75,000.00 |
| Invoice total | ₦1,075,000.00 |
| WHT at 5% on ₦1,000,000 | ₦50,000.00 |
| **You pay the consultant** | **₦1,025,000.00** |
| **You remit to the tax office** | **₦50,000.00** |

The consultant's invoice is settled in full at ₦1,075,000. They received
₦1,025,000 in cash and a credit note for ₦50,000. Both together clear the
invoice — which is why your books must show the deduction and not simply record
a short payment.

## The exemption small companies miss

A small company can often be paid in full, with nothing deducted.

To count as small, a business needs annual turnover of **₦100 million or less**
and fixed assets of **₦250 million or less**. Where it qualifies:

- Payments to it should not have WHT deducted, as long as the total paid to that
  supplier stays **under ₦2 million in the calendar month** and it has given a
  valid TIN.
- The same test works the other way round. A small company paying its own
  suppliers need not deduct, within the same monthly cap.

**The exception that catches firms:** professional, technical and consultancy
services do not qualify. A law firm, an accountancy practice, an engineering or
architecture firm still has WHT deducted from its fees however small it is.

If you are a small supplier being deducted anyway, it is usually because your
customer does not know this rule or does not have your TIN on file. Both are
worth fixing — the money is yours either way, but a credit note you have to
chase is worth less than cash you already have.

## When to remit

Two different deadlines, depending on who the tax belongs to:

- **To the Nigeria Revenue Service (federal):** by the **21st** of the month
  after the deduction.
- **To a State Internal Revenue Service:** by the **30th** of the month after
  the deduction.

Which one applies depends on the supplier. Broadly, deductions from companies go
to the federal service and deductions from individuals and unincorporated
businesses go to the state where they are resident.

This is why WHT has to be grouped by authority before you pay it over. A single
month's deductions can be owed to two or three different offices on two
different dates.

If you also charge VAT, note that the federal WHT date is the same 21st as your
[VAT return](/guides/vat-returns-nigeria). One trip, two jobs.

## Give the supplier their credit note

The deduction is only half of it. The supplier needs a **withholding tax credit
note** showing what was taken off, or they cannot claim it against their own
tax. To them, that piece of paper is the money.

Suppliers chase these constantly, and businesses that issue them promptly get a
reputation for being easy to work with. It costs nothing.

The same applies in reverse. When a customer withholds tax from what they pay
you, chase the credit note. Without it the deduction is simply money gone.

## Keeping it straight

The administration is the hard part: the right rate on each payment, the base
before VAT, the split between federal and state, the monthly deadlines, and a
credit note for every supplier.

That is what MyBooks does with it. WHT comes off supplier payments at the rate
for the transaction, grouped by authority so you can see what is owed to whom
and when, with the deduction shown on the payment advice the supplier receives.
