PAYE is the income tax an employer takes from each salary and pays over to the
tax authority for the employee. The employee owes the tax; the employer is the
one fined if it is not deducted and remitted.

From **1 January 2026** it is worked out under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025. The
bands changed, the consolidated relief allowance went, and rent relief came in.
A payroll still run on the old rules is getting every payslip wrong.

## The 2026 bands

Tax is charged on yearly taxable income, slice by slice:

| Yearly taxable income | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| First ₦800,000 | 0% |
| Next ₦2,200,000 | 15% |
| Next ₦9,000,000 | 18% |
| Next ₦13,000,000 | 21% |
| Next ₦25,000,000 | 23% |
| Above ₦50,000,000 | 25% |

Each rate applies only to the slice of income inside its band, not to the whole
salary. Someone earning ₦3,000,000 of taxable income pays nothing on the first
₦800,000 and 15% on the next ₦2,200,000.

**Minimum wage earners pay nothing.** An employee earning no more than the
national minimum wage of ₦70,000 a month has no PAYE deducted.

## What comes off before tax

Taxable income is pay less the reliefs the law allows. For most employees these
are:

- **Pension:** the employee's own contribution to an approved scheme, at least
  8% (see [payroll deductions](/guides/payroll-deductions-nigeria))
- **National Housing Fund:** the 2.5% contribution, where the employee pays it
- **Health insurance:** National Health Insurance Scheme contributions
- **Life assurance:** premiums the employee paid for themselves or their spouse
- **Mortgage interest:** interest on a loan to build or buy the home they live in
- **Rent relief:** 20% of the yearly rent they pay, up to **₦500,000**

The **consolidated relief allowance is gone.** It used to take ₦200,000 plus 20%
of gross pay off before tax. Rent relief is far narrower, and only applies when
the employee declares the rent they actually pay.

## A worked example

Amaka earns ₦500,000 a month, ₦6,000,000 a year. Her pension contribution is 8%,
and she pays ₦1,200,000 a year in rent.

| | Yearly |
| --- | --- |
| Gross pay | ₦6,000,000 |
| Less pension at 8% | ₦480,000 |
| Less rent relief: 20% of ₦1,200,000 | ₦240,000 |
| **Taxable income** | **₦5,280,000** |

Now the bands:

| Slice | Rate | Tax |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First ₦800,000 | 0% | ₦0 |
| Next ₦2,200,000 | 15% | ₦330,000 |
| Remaining ₦2,280,000 | 18% | ₦410,400 |
| **Tax for the year** | | **₦740,400** |

That is **₦61,700 a month** off her payslip: about 12.3% of her gross pay. Her
rent relief is 20% of ₦1,200,000, which is under the ₦500,000 cap. On rent of
₦2,500,000 or more she would get the full ₦500,000.

## Remitting it

- **Monthly:** PAYE deducted in a month is paid over **on or before the 10th**
  of the following month. January's PAYE is due by 10 February.
- **To the right authority:** PAYE goes to the State Internal Revenue Service
  of the state where the employee lives. Residents of the Federal Capital
  Territory are assessed by the Nigeria Revenue Service.
- **Every year:** the employer files a return of everything paid to each
  employee in the previous year **by 31 January**.

An employee who lives in one state and works in another is taxed where they
live, so a business with staff across a state border may be remitting to two
offices every month.

## Where it goes wrong

- **Old bands, old reliefs.** A spreadsheet built before 2026 still takes off
  the consolidated relief allowance and uses the old bands.
- **Rent relief with no rent declared.** It is only allowed on the rent the
  employee actually pays and declares. Keep what they give you on file.
- **Tax on a month, not a year.** The bands are yearly. Work out the year's tax
  and take a twelfth, or a raise in the middle of the year throws the months
  out.
- **Late remittance.** The 10th comes round fast, and the penalty is charged on
  tax you have already taken from your staff.

## How MyBooks does it

MyBooks runs payroll on the 2026 bands. It takes pension, NHF and rent relief
off before tax, prints a payslip for each employee, and keeps a payroll register
and a PAYE schedule to file. Change a salary in the middle of the year and the
next payslip is worked out from the new figure.
